# Getting started

About 15 minutes. You need your business hours, your main services and prices, and the phone you
answer today.

1. **Sign up** and pick a plan. The trial is free for 14 days or 50 minutes of calls.
2. **The setup wizard** asks for your business type, basics, opening hours, services, who to
   transfer calls to, and a voice. You can change everything later in **Settings** and **Assistant**.
3. **Talk to it.** At the end of the wizard, press **Test call** and talk to your assistant in the
   browser. Ask a price, book something, ask for a person.
4. **Get a phone number** in the wizard, or keep your own number and forward calls to the
   assistant ([how](call-forwarding.md)).
5. **Choose which calls it gets.** **Settings → Which calls reach your assistant?** Many businesses
   start with "Only calls we can't pick up": you answer as usual, and the assistant catches what
   you miss.
6. **Call your own number** from another phone and check that it answers.

What callers hear first: "Thanks for calling [your business], this is [name], [your business]'s
virtual assistant." It always says it's a virtual assistant. That's required by law in many places,
and callers appreciate it.

## After setup

Your overview shows a **Getting started** list: a test call, your number, a first real call, an
answer of your own, your calendar, your team and the data processing agreement. Each step ticks
itself off once it's done. Hide it whenever you like; **Help → Getting started checklist** brings
it back.

The first time you open the dashboard after setup, a one-minute tour shows you where everything is.
**Help → Take the tour** replays it, and **Help → Guides** has every article, including one for each
role on your team ([roles](team-and-roles.md)).
